    Decimal¶

    A Decimal is an exact decimal number. Use it for money, prices, rates and any amount that must add up to the cent. A number written with a decimal point is a Decimal, and arithmetic on it has no binary rounding error:

    0.1 + 0.2 == 0.3

    true

    A Decimal is an integer significand of up to 512 bits, about 154 digits, and a scale: the number of digits after the point, from 0 to 154. The scale is kept for display. A sum or a difference has the larger scale of its operands, and a product the sum of their scales. Equality and order are by value, so 1.50 == 1.5 is true:

    [1.50, 1.50 + 1.25, 1.50 * 1.5]

    [1.50, 2.75, 2.250]

    A literal keeps every digit it is written with, however many there are:

    123456789012345678901234567890.123456789 + 0.000000001

    123456789012345678901234567890.123456790

    Division and rounding¶

    / is exact where the quotient terminates, and otherwise rounded half to even to 100 significant digits. Division by zero is an execute failure, as it is for an Int. round(x, places) rounds half to even to places digits after the point, and round(x) to a whole number:

    [10 / 4.0, round(2 / 3.0, 4), round(2.345, 2), round(2.355, 2), round(2.5)]

    [2.5, 0.6667, 2.34, 2.36, 2]

    Int, Float and Decimal¶

    An Int and a Decimal combine directly. A Float combines with neither, and every conversion to or from it is written:

    Arithmetic and comparison take an Int and a Decimal together. Arithmetic gives a Decimal, computed as if the Int were a Decimal of scale 0:

    [12.50 * 3, 7 - 0.25]

    [37.50, 6.75]

    A Float does not mix with a Decimal. A binary float has no exact decimal value, so the program must choose the conversion. decimal(x) reads an Int, or a Float by its shortest digits, as a Decimal; float(d) gives the nearest Float and int(d) the integer part:

    1.5 + 2.0f

    check error[type-mismatch]: a Decimal and a Float do not mix: write `decimal(x)` to read the Float as a Decimal, or `float(d)` to read the Decimal as a Float
 --> <expression>:1:7
  |
1 | 1.5 + 2.0f
  |       ^^^^

    [decimal(0.1f) + 1.5, "12.50".to_decimal().unwrap_or(0.0)]

    [1.6, 12.50]

    JSON, YAML and CSV carry a Decimal without loss: a number read as a Decimal field keeps the digits it is written with, and a Decimal is written as a number with its digits and scale.
